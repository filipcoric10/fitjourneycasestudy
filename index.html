Hello reader,
Have you ever started a training plan, missed one week and never opened the app again? Most fitness apps are built for the perfect week: a daily streak, a full program and a plan that assumes you always have 60 minutes and full energy. Real weeks are different. Some days you have 25 minutes, some days you have no energy at all.
Fit Journey is an iOS app I designed for people who want to keep training through those weeks. It does not ask for perfection. It asks you to show up, even for a short session, and it helps with the rest: what to lift next time, how your form looks and who is training with you. In this case study I will show you how I got there.

01 · THE PROBLEM
Training plans fail on the bad weeks.
Looking at how people around me train, and at my own habits, I kept coming back to four problems:
One missed day breaks everything
Daily streaks reset to zero. After a sick week or a holiday, starting again feels like failing again.
No time, no energy, no workout
The plan says 60 minutes. You have 25 and you are tired, so you skip it completely.
Not knowing what to lift next
Without notes from last time, people repeat the same weight for months or guess.
Training alone, with no feedback
Nobody checks your form and nobody notices if you stop coming.
02 · RESEARCH
What the best apps already do
I studied three popular apps. Each one solves part of the problem very well, and each one leaves a gap for someone who trains on and off.
Fitbod
DOES WELL
Builds a workout from your equipment and recovery, and remembers the weights you lifted.
WHAT I MISSED
The plan assumes a normal day. There is no easy path for a day when you have little time or energy.
Strava
DOES WELL
Friends, kudos, clubs and monthly challenges keep people moving together.
WHAT I MISSED
It is built around running and cycling. Gym sets and weights are not the focus.
Apple Fitness+
DOES WELL
Guided classes that you can filter by length, from short to long.
WHAT I MISSED
Classes are video-led. You do not log your own sets or see what to lift next time.
Fit Journey takes the best of each: Fitbod’s memory for weights, Strava’s friends and challenges, and Fitness+’s choice of length. From this I wrote three goals that every screen had to pass:
Continuity over perfection
A short workout counts. A missed day never resets your progress.
No load on the user
Pre-filled sets, one fix at a time, short copy, nothing to set up.
Support at every step
A coach for form and food, friends for motivation, gentle reminders.
03 · WHO IT IS FOR
Meet Michael
Fit Journey is for everyone from teenagers to adults who want to train regularly but whose weeks never look the same. They are not athletes and they do not want a perfect body. They want to feel better and stop starting over. Michael is one of them.
Michael
31 · Entrepreneur
Works every day
ABOUT
Runs his own business and works every day. Some days he has an hour and plenty of energy for training, other days he has much less of both.
GOALS
Keep training regularly, whatever the week looks like. On a busy day, even 25 minutes is worth it.
FRUSTRATIONS
His schedule is never the same two days in a row, so a fixed training plan does not fit his life.
04 · STRUCTURE
82 screens in six flows
Before polishing any UI, I mapped every screen and every path between them. Home, Train, Challenges and Profile live in the tab bar. The AI Coach sits in the middle of the tab bar, one tap away from any screen. Every back and close button has a defined destination, and short messages that disappear on their own return the user to the screen they came from.

05 · KEY DECISIONS
Seven decisions that keep people training
DECISION 1
A weekly goal instead of a daily streak
A daily streak punishes one missed day. Fit Journey counts workouts per week instead: three workouts on any days complete the week, and a missed Tuesday changes nothing.
The goal sits at the top of Home as a half-circle of dots: blue for you, coral for the friend you are dueling with.

DECISION 2
A plan for low energy days
The Train tab starts with one question: how much time do you have today? 25, 45, 60 or 90 minutes, each with its own intensity.
On days when even that feels like too much, "Low energy?" offers three small options: an easy 25 minute gym session, 20 minutes at home with no equipment, or a 30 minute walk. All three count toward the week.
DECISION 3
Logging a set takes one tap
Every set is pre-filled with the weight and reps from last time. If nothing changed, the user only taps "Done set".
After the last set the app asks one question: go heavier next time? The user picks a step (+1, +2.5 or +5 kg) per exercise, and the next workout already shows the new weight.

DECISION 4
Form check with one fix, not ten
The user films a set from the side. The AI Coach watches each rep and returns a score out of 10 and one correction, the one that matters most for safety.
If the body is not in frame, the app explains how to film again instead of guessing.
DECISION 5
Snap any equipment, get exercise ideas
At a new gym or at home, the user points the camera at a machine, kettlebell or rope. The coach suggests three exercises and can turn them into a 20 minute set.
The equipment is saved to that place, so the next workout there already knows what is available.
Point at any machine, ball or rope.

DECISION 6
Friendly duels instead of leaderboards
A duel is between the user and one or more friends: workouts, active minutes, steps or kilometres over one week, two weeks or a month.
Everyone sees everyone’s progress week by week, and a cheer can be sent with one tap. The goal is to push each other to train, not to rank strangers.
DECISION 7
Drink reminders that accept tea
Water is important, but not everyone likes plain water all day. The reminder offers water, tea, lemonade or electrolytes, and the amount: 250, 330 or 500 ml.
The user chooses how often reminders come and when they stop, and the Home card fills like a glass.

06 · USABILITY TESTING
What people said after using the prototype
I tested the clickable prototype with people who train on and off. Three things came up again and again.
The app motivates instead of judging
The strongest reaction was about how the app made them feel. Testers said it makes them want to train, not feel guilty about the days they missed.
Low energy days are where it wins
This is the moment they would normally skip. Seeing a 25 minute, a home and a walk option made it easy to do something instead of nothing.
Duels are the biggest driver
Friendly duels were the feature that motivated them the most. They pictured themselves pushing a friend, and being pushed back, to do at least some training every day.
07 · VISUAL DESIGN
Clean, white and full of color
Training should feel light and friendly, not like a performance test. The base is clean white, so every screen stays calm. Each part of the app has its own color from the rainbow, which makes categories easy to tell apart, while green is reserved for actions and the tab bar.

Rounded letters and friendly numbers. Big numbers carry the progress, so the type had to make them feel encouraging.

16 px side margins, 8 px buttons and 16 px cards on every screen. One rounded icon set (Tabler) with the same stroke everywhere. All spacing uses whole pixels.

09 · FINAL THOUGHTS
What I learned
Tone is
a feature
The same data can motivate or judge. Short, kind copy and a weekly goal did more for motivation than any extra feature.
Less input, more support
Pre-filled sets and one fix at a time keep the app out of the way, so the user can focus on training.
People train for people
Testers cared most about training with friends. Social features work best when they are personal, not public.
Next, I would test the AI Coach answers with a real trainer and run a longer test to see how many people are still training after a month.
Thank you for reading.
If you have a question or would like to work together, write to me.
